## Corrections to my notes

- **Five Emperors** — in Sima Qian's *Shiji* (the list quizbowl uses) they are the **Yellow Emperor,
  Zhuanxu, Di Ku, Yao, and Shun**. Shaohao is a Five Emperor only in other lists, so "Shaohao was the
  first of the Five Emperors" is a minority version.
- **Jingwei is not Nüwa the creator.** The Jingwei bird was the Yan Emperor's daughter, whose name is
  also read *Nüwa* (女娃, a different character from 女媧). Quizbowl has garbled this at least once.
- **Zhu Bajie's names** — *Bajie* means **"Eight Precepts"** (given by Xuanzang). "Pig Awakened to
  Power/Ability" is his dharma name **Zhu Wuneng** (given by Guanyin).
- **Zhu Bajie at Gao Village** is usually exposed by his **appetite** (he eats more than the farm
  produces) and his pig face, not by drink.
- **Houji** — the ancestor of the Zhou, credited with millet; he served **Yao and Shun** (and helped Yu),
  so "during the Xia" is loose. **Agriculturalism** (*Nongjia*) claimed **Shennong**, not Houji.
- **Great Learning** — attributed to Confucius and his disciple **Zengzi** (Zeng Shen). Zeng Xi was
  Zengzi's father.
- **Thirteen Classics** — fixed as a canon under the **Song**. The Ming and Qing exams were set on the
  **Four Books and Five Classics** (with Zhu Xi's commentaries), not the Thirteen Classics.
- ***Classic of Mountains and Seas*** — edited for the Han library by **Liu Xin**, Liu Xiang's son.
  Liu Xiang himself compiled the *Strategies of the Warring States* and *Biographies of Exemplary Women*.
- ***Romance of the Three Kingdoms* opening line** ("The empire, long divided, must unite…") was added
  in **Mao Zonggang's 1679 edition**, the standard text, not by Luo Guanzhong.
- **Guan Yu's death** — captured by **Sun Quan's** general **Lü Meng** and executed by the Wu (my Three
  Kingdoms *history* notes say Cao Cao; the literature notes have it right).

## Mythology

### Creation
- ★ **Pangu** slept and grew for **18,000 years** inside the cosmic egg, then held heaven and earth
  apart for another 18,000. At his death his **breath became the wind**, his **voice thunder**, his
  **eyes the sun and moon**, his blood the rivers, and the **parasites/fleas on his body humanity**. The
  first written Pangu story is **Xu Zheng's** *Sanwu Liji* (3rd c., Three Kingdoms Wu) — late, so
  Pangu is probably borrowed from southern peoples.
- ★ **Nüwa (Nügua)** made people **by hand from yellow earth**, tired, then **dragged a rope through mud**
  and flung the drops. The hand-made became the **rich/nobles**, the rope-made the **poor/commoners**.
  This version is in **Ying Shao's** *Fengsu Tongyi*; the sky-mending is in the ***Huainanzi***.
- ★ **Mending the heavens:** Nüwa melted **five-coloured stones** to patch the sky, cut the **legs of
  the giant turtle Ao** for the four pillars, **killed a black dragon**, and piled **reed ashes** to dam
  the flood. The damage came when the water god **Gonggong** butted **Mount Buzhou**, a pillar of
  heaven, after losing to **Zhuanxu** — or to the fire god **Zhurong** in other versions. The sky tilted
  northwest and the earth sank southeast, which is **why Chinese rivers flow east** and the stars drift west.
- **Nüwa and Fuxi** are drawn with **intertwined snake tails**, holding a **compass and set square**
  (Han tomb reliefs; Tang silk paintings from **Astana**, Turfan). Their mother **Huaxu** conceived them
  by stepping in a giant's (the thunder god's) footprint. They married on **Mount Kunlun** after a flood
  (a Miao version: they survive the flood in a **gourd** grown from the thunder god's tooth).
- **Fuxi** taught **fishing nets**, hunting, cooking, and marriage. The **Yellow River Map (Hetu)** rose
  from the river on a **dragon-horse** and inspired his trigrams. The **Luo River Writing (Luoshu)** came
  on a turtle's back to Yu. **King Wen of Zhou** doubled the trigrams into the 64 hexagrams of the *I Ching*.

### The sage kings
- ★ **Shennong ("Divine Farmer")**, ox-headed, **tasted a hundred herbs** (poisoned seventy times in a
  day), had a **transparent stomach**, invented the **plough** (*leisi*), taught **farming** and markets,
  and named the **Divine Farmer's Materia Medica** (*Shennong Bencao Jing*). Conflated with **Yan Di**,
  the Flame Emperor. Credited with the **guqin**.
- ★ **Yellow Emperor (Huangdi; Xuanyuan)**:
  - **Battle of Banquan** — the *first* battle — he beat the Yan Emperor, and the two clans merged
    into the Huaxia (the source of "descendants of Yan and Huang").
  - **Battle of Zhuolu** vs **Chiyou** — he built the **south-pointing chariot** to navigate Chiyou's
    fog. The winged dragon **Yinglong** and his daughter, the drought goddess **Ba (Nüba)**, won it; a
    drum of the one-legged **Kui's** hide beaten with a thunder beast's bone scared Chiyou's army.
  - **Xingtian**, beheaded by Huangdi, fights on with **nipples for eyes and navel for a mouth**.
  - He cast a tripod at **Mount Jing** and **ascended to heaven on a dragon**; his mausoleum is at
    **Huangling, Shaanxi**. His name is on the ***Huangdi Neijing*** (*Inner Classic*), the founding text
    of Chinese medicine and acupuncture, and on **Huang–Lao** Daoism.
  - Wife **Leizu**: a silkworm cocoon fell into her tea and unravelled. Scribe **Cangjie** (four-eyed)
    invented writing — **millet rained from the sky and ghosts wailed** at night.
- ★ **Yao** passed over his own son **Danzhu** and gave the throne to **Shun**, with his two daughters
  **Ehuang and Nüying** as wives. They became the goddesses of the **Xiang River**; their tears made the
  **spotted (Xiangfei) bamboo**. **Shun** was famed for **filial piety** toward a father and stepbrother
  who tried to kill him. He banished the **Four Perils** (Gonggong among them) and abdicated to Yu — the
  model of **abdication by merit (*shanrang*)**.
- ★ **The Great Flood:**
  - Yao, advised by the **Four Mountains** (*Siyue*), appointed **Gun**. Gun stole the self-expanding
    soil **xirang** from heaven and built dams; the flood won. He was **executed by Zhurong on Feather
    Mountain (Yushan)**; his corpse did not rot for three years, and **Yu** was born from his belly.
  - **Yu the Great** dredged **channels instead of dams** for **13 years** and **passed his own door
    three times without going in**. Helpers: **Yinglong** (traced channels with his tail), a **black
    turtle**, **Houji**, and **Boyi**. He killed **Xiangliu**, Gonggong's **nine-headed serpent**
    minister, cut the **Dragon Gate (Longmen)**, and cast the **Nine Tripods (*Jiuding*)** for the nine
    provinces — the later symbols of the mandate.
  - **Carp that leap the Dragon Gate become dragons** — the idiom said to exam candidates.

### Moon, sun, and stars
- ★ **The ten suns** were sons of **Di Jun** and **Xihe**, **three-legged crows (*sanzuwu*)** roosting in
  the **Fusang** tree; they rose all at once. Emperor Yao sent **(Hou) Yi**, who shot nine. Yi also
  killed monsters (the giant boar **Fengxi**, the **Xiushe** snake, the wind-bird **Dafeng**).
- ★ **Yi's death:** his apprentice **Feng Meng** (Peng Meng), jealous after losing an archery contest,
  **clubbed him to death with peach wood**.
- ★ **Chang'e** (Heng'e; Chang-O) took the **elixir from the Queen Mother of the West** — a dose for two —
  and floated to the moon, to the **Guanghan Palace**. In the *Huainanzi* version she becomes a **toad**.
  ★ The **Jade Rabbit (Yutu; Tu'er Ye)** pounds the elixir with a **mortar and pestle**. ★ **Wu Gang**,
  the "Chinese Sisyphus", fells a **self-healing osmanthus (cassia)** tree forever — punishment for
  neglecting his Daoist study. China's lunar probes are named **Chang'e** and its rovers **Yutu**.
- ★ **Qu Yuan's "Heavenly Questions" (*Tianwen*)** asks why Yi shot the **Earl of the River (Hebo)** and
  took his bride, the **Luo River goddess (Luoshen/Fufei)**.
- ★ **Cowherd (Niulang) and Weaver Girl (Zhinü):**
  - His **talking ox** told him where she bathed; when the ox died, he wore its **hide** to fly after her.
  - In some versions it is the **Queen Mother of the West** who scratches the **Milky Way** with her
    **hairpin**. They meet on the **magpie bridge** on the **seventh night of the seventh month** —
    **Qixi**; Korea's **Chilseok** and Japan's **Tanabata**.
  - One of the **Four Great Folktales**, with the **White Snake**, **Lady Meng Jiang** (who wept down the
    Great Wall), and the **Butterfly Lovers** (Liang Shanbo and Zhu Yingtai).
- **White Snake:** **Bai Suzhen** and her maid **Xiaoqing** (green snake) at **West Lake**, Hangzhou — they
  meet **Xu Xian** at the **Broken Bridge**. **Realgar wine** at the Dragon Boat Festival reveals her
  form. She **floods Jinshan Temple** to free him, and the monk **Fahai** imprisons her under
  **Leifeng Pagoda**.

### Heaven, immortals, and household gods
- ★ **Jade Emperor (Yu Huang, Yudi, Tian Gong)** — top of the **celestial bureaucracy**. Above him in
  Daoism are the **Three Pure Ones (*Sanqing*)**: **Yuanshi Tianzun** (Jade Purity), **Lingbao
  Tianzun**, and **Daode Tianzun** = **Taishang Laojun**, the deified **Laozi** (the lowest of the three).
- ★ **Queen Mother of the West (Xiwangmu)** — palace on ★ **Mount Kunlun** (an *axis mundi*, by the
  **Jasper Lake**). Her **peaches of immortality** ripen every **3,000 years**, and the **Peach Banquet
  (*Pantao hui*)** is the feast the Eight Immortals travel to and Sun Wukong ruins. In the
  *Shanhaijing* she is wild, with a **leopard's tail and tiger's teeth**. She meets **King Mu of Zhou**
  (*Mu Tianzi Zhuan*) and teaches **Emperor Wu of Han** (*Han Wudi Neizhuan*). Consort: the **King
  Father of the East (Dongwanggong)**; popular myth makes her the Jade Emperor's wife.
- ★ **Eight Immortals (*Baxian*; Pa Hsien)** on ★ **Penglai** (one of three isles of the immortals in
  the **Bohai Sea**):
  - **Lü Dongbin** — sword; the **Yellow Millet Dream** (a lifetime dreamed while millet cooks, shown
    to him by Zhongli Quan).
  - **Zhongli Quan** — fan; a Han general.
  - **Li Tieguai (Iron-Crutch Li)** — gourd of medicine.
  - **Zhang Guolao** — rides a **white donkey backwards** and folds it into his pocket; first appeared
    as a **white bat** and was **advisor to Yao** in a past life.
  - **Han Xiangzi** — **flute**; nephew of the Tang writer **Han Yu**.
  - **He Xiangu** — the only woman; lotus; immortal by eating **powdered mica/mother-of-pearl**.
  - **Lan Caihe** — **gender-ambiguous**; flower basket.
  - **Cao Guojiu** — castanets; a Song imperial uncle.
  - Stock story: **"The Eight Immortals Cross the Sea,"** each by their own power, fighting the Dragon King.
  - **Xu Fu**, Qin Shi Huang's *fangshi*, sailed east for Penglai's elixir and supposedly ended up in
    **Japan** (calling **Mount Fuji** Penglai).
- ★ **Kitchen God (Zao Jun; Zao Shen; Stove God; Zhang Lang)**:
  - Story: **Zhang Lang** left his wife for another woman, went blind and became a beggar. His ex-wife
    fed him; on recognising her he **threw himself into the hearth** and burned.
  - He reports to the Jade Emperor on the **23rd/24th of the twelfth month ("Little New Year")**, so his
    lips are smeared with **honey or maltose** and his **paper image is burned** to send him up.
    ***Nian gao*** (sticky cake) glues his mouth shut.
- ★ **Mazu (Lin Moniang; Tianhou "Empress of Heaven", Tianfei; Tin Hau)** — **sea goddess** from
  **Meizhou Island, Fujian** (Song dynasty). As a girl she **saved her father and brothers in a storm
  while in a trance**. Protector of sailors (credited with protecting **Zheng He**). Attendants:
  **Thousand-Li Eye and Wind-Following Ear**. Hugely venerated in **Taiwan**.
- **Caishen** (god of wealth; **Zhao Gongming**); **Fu, Lu, Shou** (fortune, prosperity, longevity).
- **Leigong** (thunder god, with drums) and his wife **Dianmu** (lightning, holding **mirrors**) punish
  hidden crimes.
- **Zhong Kui** — demon-queller. He took first in the imperial exam but was refused for his **ugliness**
  and killed himself; Tang **Xuanzong** dreamed of him. Painted on **doors** at New Year.

### Fengshen Yanyi, dragons, and Nezha
- ★ ***Investiture of the Gods (Fengshen Yanyi)***:
  - A Ming **gods-and-demons (*shenmo*)** novel, attributed to **Xu Zhonglin**.
  - **King Zhou of Shang (Di Xin)** writes a lustful poem on **Nüwa's temple** wall, so she sends a
    **nine-tailed fox spirit** to possess **Daji**.
  - Daji's torture, the ***paolao*** — a heated bronze pillar ("Bronze Toaster"). The **wine pool and
    meat forest**.
  - **Jiang Ziya (Jiang Taigong)** fished with a **straight hook** until **King Wen** hired him. He holds
    the **List of Investiture** that deifies the war dead.
- ★ **Fox spirits — *huli jing***, often **nine-tailed** (from the *Shanhaijing's* **Qingqiu**). Korean
  ***kumiho***, Japanese ***kitsune***. Many in **Pu Songling's *Strange Stories from a Chinese Studio***.
- ★ **Nezha**:
  - Born after a **three-and-a-half-year (42-month) pregnancy** as a **ball of flesh**, which his father
    **Li Jing** (later the **Pagoda-Bearing Heavenly King**) split with a sword.
  - Bathing his **Red Armillary Sash** stirred the East Sea; he killed the yaksha **Li Gen** and **Ao
    Bing**, third son of the Dragon King.
  - He **cut away his own flesh and bones** to repay his parents. His master **Taiyi Zhenren** rebuilt
    him from **lotus roots**.
  - **Wind Fire Wheels**, fire-tipped spear, **Universe Ring**; the "Third Lotus Prince."
- ★ **Dragon Kings of the Four Seas (*Longwang*)** — **Ao Guang** (East; gave Sun Wukong his staff),
  Ao Qin (South), **Ao Run** (West; father of the **White Dragon Horse**), Ao Shun (North). Chinese
  dragons bring **rain**, rule waters, and are benevolent (the zodiac's only mythical animal).
  **Yinglong** is the winged dragon of rain.
- **Erlang Shen** — **three-eyed** god with the **Howling Celestial Dog** (*Xiaotian Quan*), which bit
  Sun Wukong's calf. Erlang captured Wukong; Wukong hid as a **temple with his tail as a flagpole**.
  Linked to **Li Bing**, builder of **Dujiangyan**.
- ***Classic of Mountains and Seas (Shanhaijing)*** — mythic geography/bestiary, credited by tradition
  to **Yu and Boyi**, with commentary by **Guo Pu** (Jin). Home of **Xingtian**, **Dijiang** (a faceless,
  winged sack — Hundun), the **nine-tailed fox**, **Jingwei**, **Kuafu**, and the earliest Xiwangmu.
- **Jingwei** — the Yan Emperor's daughter drowned in the Eastern Sea and became a bird that **fills
  the sea with twigs and pebbles**, a symbol of hopeless perseverance.

### Underworld and festivals
- ★ **Diyu** is run by **Yanluo Wang (Yama)** and the **Ten Kings of Hell**. **Meng Po** serves the **Soup
  of Forgetfulness** at the **Naihe Bridge** before rebirth. **Hungry ghosts** are fed at the **Ghost
  Festival** (15th of the 7th month). ***Jiangshi*** are hopping corpses that suck *qi*.
- ★ **Nian** — a beast that came at New Year, scared off by **red**, noise, and fire. That is the origin of
  **firecrackers, red couplets (*chunlian*)**, the **lion and dragon dances**, and **red envelopes**. The
  **upside-down *fu*** (福倒 *fu dao* = "fortune arrives"). New Year ends with the **Lantern Festival**.
- ★ **Great Race, full order** (rat, ox, tiger, rabbit, dragon, snake, horse, goat, monkey, rooster,
  dog, pig):
  - ox — carried the rat across the river, which jumped off ahead
  - tiger — swam hard
  - rabbit — **hopped stones / rode a log**
  - dragon — **stopped to make rain** (or to push the rabbit's log)
  - snake — **hid on the horse's hoof**
  - goat, monkey, rooster — **shared a raft**
  - dog — **played in the river**
  - pig — **ate and napped**
  - The **cat** was pushed off the ox (or not woken) by the rat, hence their feud. The **Vietnamese**
    zodiac has a **cat for the rabbit** and a **water buffalo for the ox**.
- **Four Symbols** — **Azure Dragon (Qinglong)** east/spring/wood; **Vermilion Bird (Zhuque)**
  south/summer/fire; **White Tiger (Baihu)** west/autumn/metal; **Black Tortoise (Xuanwu)**
  north/winter/water, drawn as a **tortoise entwined with a snake**. Japan's **Seiryū** etc.; painted in
  **Goguryeo tombs**.
- **Wu Xing planets** — wood Jupiter, fire Mars, earth Saturn, **metal Venus**, water Mercury.
  Generating cycle: wood → fire → earth → metal → water.

### Buddhist figures (headings in my notes)
- ★ **Guanyin** = **Avalokiteśvara**, the bodhisattva of compassion, made **female** in China. Legend of
  **Princess Miaoshan**, who gave her **arms and eyes** to cure her father (hence the
  **Thousand-Armed** Guanyin). Island of **Putuoshan**. Hidden-Christian **"Maria Kannon"** statues in
  Japan. In *Journey to the West* she sets up the pilgrimage and gives the **headband**.
- **Ksitigarbha (Dizang)** — vowed not to become a Buddha **until the hells are empty**; Japan's **Jizō**
  protects children and travellers.
- **Four Heavenly Kings** — **Vaiśravaṇa/Duowen** (north; **pagoda**; Japan's **Bishamonten**, with
  spear and pagoda), Virūḍhaka (south; sword), Virūpākṣa (west; serpent), Dhṛtarāṣṭra (east; *pipa*).
- **Laughing Buddha (Budai)** — a fat Later Liang monk with a **cloth sack**, seen as an incarnation of
  **Maitreya**; Japan's **Hotei**, one of the **Seven Lucky Gods**.

## Literature — Four Great Classical Novels

### ★ *Romance of the Three Kingdoms (Sanguo Yanyi)*
- The **earliest** of the four. Based on **Chen Shou's** 3rd-century history ***Records of the Three
  Kingdoms (Sanguozhi)*** — *don't confuse them*; the *Sanguozhi* also has the first account of the
  shaman queen **Himiko**. A Yuan precursor is the ***Sanguozhi Pinghua***.
- Opens with the **Yellow Turban Rebellion** (led by **Zhang Jue**, "Way of Supreme Peace") and the
  eunuch **Ten Attendants** (led by **Zhang Rang**); ends with **Sima Yan's Jin** conquering Wu (280).
- ★ **Cao Cao (Mengde)**:
  - **"I'd rather betray the world than let the world betray me"** — after killing **Lü Boshe's**
    household, mistaking knives being sharpened (to kill a pig for his dinner) for a murder plot.
  - Troops told of **imaginary sour plums** to make them salivate; the **"chicken ribs" (*jilei*)**
    password, which **Yang Xiu** read correctly and was executed for.
  - **"The only heroes in the world are you and I"** — Liu Bei drops his chopsticks and blames the
    **thunder**.
  - Dies of headaches after refusing **Hua Tuo's** offer to **open his skull** (and jailing him).
    Built the **Bronze Sparrow Terrace**. Son **Cao Pi** founded Wei.
  - Son **Cao Zhi**'s **Seven Steps Poem** — beans boiled over a fire of their own stalks.
- ★ **Liu Bei (Xuande)** — the hero; Imperial Uncle; horse **Dilu** leaps the **Tan Stream**.
  - **Zhao Yun** rescues his infant son **Liu Shan (A Dou)** at **Changban**, while **Lady Mi** throws
    herself down a well. Liu Bei **throws the baby on the ground** for nearly costing him a general.
  - **Five Tiger Generals:** Guan Yu, Zhang Fei, **Zhao Yun, Ma Chao, Huang Zhong**.
- ★ **Guan Yu (Yunchang)**:
  - **Green Dragon Crescent Blade**; **spares Cao Cao at Huarong Trail** after Red Cliffs.
  - Later deified as **Guandi/Guan Gong**, god of war (and wealth), worshipped by **police and triads**
    alike; **Sangharama Bodhisattva** in Buddhism.
  - His ghost **possesses and kills Lü Meng** at a banquet.
- **Zhang Fei** flogs a corrupt inspector; is murdered in his sleep by his own officers.
- ★ **Zhuge Liang (Kongming), "Sleeping/Crouching Dragon (Wolong)"**:
  - Wrote the **Longzhong Plan**.
  - **"Borrows the east wind"** with a three-day ritual at the **Seven Stars Altar**.
  - **Captures and releases Meng Huo seven times** (the Nanman); the **Empty Fort Strategy** — plays
    the **guqin** on the open city wall at **Xicheng** to scare off **Sima Yi**.
  - The **Chu Shi Biao** (memorials on the Northern Expeditions); dies at the **Wuzhang Plains** (234).
  - His **wooden statue** scares Sima Yi off ("a dead Zhuge frightens a living Zhongda").
  - Credited with **Kongming lanterns**, the **wooden ox**, the **repeating crossbow**, and (legend) the
    **mantou** bun.
- **Pang Tong, "Young/Fledgling Phoenix"** — suggests the **chained ships** to Cao Cao (the **Chain
  Stratagem**); killed at **Fallen Phoenix Slope**.
- ★ **Red Cliffs (Chibi), 208**:
  - Plan by **Zhou Yu** (jealous of Zhuge: "Since Heaven made Yu, why make Liang too?").
  - **Huang Gai's self-injury ruse** — flogged, fake defection, fire ships. The straw-boat **borrowed
    arrows**.
  - **Su Shi's** *Former Ode on the Red Cliff* is about the site.
- ★ **Lü Bu**:
  - Kills **two adoptive fathers** (Ding Yuan, then **Dong Zhuo**).
  - Red Hare: "Among men, **Lü Bu**; among horses, **Red Hare**."
  - Caught at **Xiapi** when the city is flooded; hanged at the **White Gate Tower**.
- **Xiahou Dun** pulls an arrow from his eye and **eats the eyeball**. Liu Shan, captive in Wei: so happy
  he "doesn't think of Shu" (*le bu si Shu*).

### ★ *Water Margin (Shuihu Zhuan; Outlaws of the Marsh; All Men Are Brothers)*
- Versions: **70-chapter** (Jin Shengtan's cut), 100, and 120 ("simple" vs "complex" editions). Sometimes
  co-credited to **Luo Guanzhong**. Based on a real Huizong-era outlaw **Song Jiang**, first written up
  in ***Old Incidents of the Xuanhe Era***. Buck's title is from the *Analects* ("within the four seas,
  **all men are brothers**").
- ★ **Song Jiang, "Timely Rain" (Opportune Rain)**:
  - Kills his blackmailing concubine **Yan Poxi**; is visited by the **Mystic Queen of the Ninth Heaven**.
  - **Accepts amnesty**, then fights the **Liao** and **Fang La** — two-thirds of the band die.
  - Is killed with **poisoned imperial wine** (he makes **Li Kui** drink too).
- ★ **Wu Song** — "after three bowls, don't cross the ridge": drinks **18 bowls**, kills the tiger on
  **Jingyang Ridge**. Avenges his brother **Wu Dalang**, a **dwarf who sold flatbread**, poisoned by
  **Pan Jinlian** and **Ximen Qing**.
- **Lin Chong, "Panther Head"** — drill instructor framed by Gao Qiu for **carrying a sword into the White
  Tiger Hall**; the **Mountain God Temple** in the snow.
- **Lu Zhishen, "Flowery (Tattooed) Monk"** — kills the butcher **"Lord of the West" (Zheng)** with three
  punches, **uproots a willow**.
- **Li Kui, "Black Whirlwind" / "Iron Ox"** — twin axes; fights **Zhang Shun, "White Streak in the
  Waves,"** in the water.
- **Sun Erniang "the Ogress" and Zhang Qing "the Gardener"** — the **Cross Slope** inn that serves
  **human-flesh buns**.
- **Dai Zong, "Magic Traveller,"** ties **talismans to his legs**. **Wu Yong** — strategist.
  **Gongsun Sheng** — Daoist magician. **Shi Qian** steals the **goose-feather armour** and a **rooster**,
  starting the **Zhu Family Village** attacks.
- **Chao Gai**, the first leader, dies of an arrow. A **stone tablet** in the **Loyalty Hall** ranks
  all 108. Only **three women**, including **Hu Sanniang** and Sun Erniang.
- Saying: **"The young shouldn't read *Water Margin*; the old shouldn't read *Three Kingdoms*."**
- **Kuniyoshi's** 1827 print series *108 Heroes of the Popular Suikoden* → the **full-body tattoo** fad.
- ★ ***Plum in the Golden Vase (Jin Ping Mei; The Golden Lotus)***:
  - By the pseudonymous **"Scoffing Scholar of Lanling."** The title takes one character from each of
    **Pan Jinlian, Li Ping'er, and Pang Chunmei**.
  - **Ximen Qing** dies of an **aphrodisiac overdose**.
  - Translated by **David Tod Roy**.

### ★ *Journey to the West (Xiyouji; Monkey)*
- **100 chapters**, published 1592; attribution to **Wu Cheng'en** is traditional, not certain. The real
  **Xuanzang** left in 629 and returned in 645; he wrote the ***Great Tang Records on the Western Regions***.
  The novel has the emperor **Taizong** send him.
- **Tang Sanzang / Tang Seng / Tripitaka** — reincarnation of **Golden Cicada**; demons want to **eat his
  flesh for immortality**.
- ★ **Sun Wukong ("Aware of Vacuity")**:
  - Taught by **Patriarch Subhuti**, found through a **woodcutter's song**; Stone Monkey; **Handsome
    Monkey King**.
  - **Erases names from the Book of Life and Death**. Made **Bimawen, Keeper of the Heavenly Horses** —
    an insult — so he frees the horses and calls himself **Great Sage Equal to Heaven (Qitian Dasheng)**.
  - **Havoc in Heaven**: eats **Laozi's pills** and the **peaches**. **49 days in Laozi's Eight Trigrams
    Furnace** give him **"fiery eyes and golden pupils"** that see through disguises (but are weak
    against smoke). Captured by **Erlang Shen**.
  - **Writes "The Great Sage was here" and urinates on a pillar** — the Buddha's finger — then is
    sealed under **Five Elements Mountain**.
  - Controlled by the **golden headband** and the **Tightening Spell ("Headache Sutra")**. A
    **108,000-li cloud somersault**; **hairs into clones**.
  - Fights the **Six-Eared Macaque** double. Becomes the **Victorious Fighting Buddha**.
- ★ Episodes:
  - **White Bone Demon** — disguised as girl, mother, then father; Xuanzang banishes Wukong for
    "murder."
  - **Princess Iron Fan** and the **Bull Demon King** — her **banana-leaf fan** puts out the **Flaming
    Mountains**; their son **Red Boy**.
  - **Kingdom of Women** — **pregnancy from river water**. **Spider spirits**. **Cart-Slow Kingdom** —
    contest with three Daoist magicians.
  - At **Vulture Peak** they first get **blank scriptures** (no bribe paid). The **81st tribulation**: the
    **old turtle** dumps them in the river because they forgot his question.
- ★ **Zhu Bajie** — formerly **Marshal Tianpeng (Heavenly Canopy)**, who drunkenly harassed **Chang'e**;
  "the Idiot"; ends as **Cleanser of the Altars** (eats the leftovers). **Sha Wujing** — formerly the
  **Curtain-Lifting General**; **flogged 800 times** and stabbed by flying swords every 7 days; necklace of
  **nine skulls** (of past pilgrims); lives in the **Flowing Sands River**; ends an **arhat**. **White
  Dragon Horse** — set fire to his father's **pearl**, ate Xuanzang's horse.
- Afterlives:
  - Waley's abridged ***Monkey*** (1942); **Anthony Yu's** complete translation; **Julia Lovell's
    *Monkey King*** (2021).
  - The *Supplement* (***Xiyoubu***) traps Wukong in a **fish demon's dream world**.
  - **Gene Luen Yang's *American Born Chinese***; **Maxine Hong Kingston's *Tripmaster Monkey***.
  - *Dragon Ball*'s **Son Goku**; the 2024 game ***Black Myth: Wukong***.

### ★ *Dream of the Red Chamber (Hong Lou Meng; The Story of the Stone)*
- **Cao Xueqin** (d. 1763) — his family were ruined Jiangning textile commissioners. **120 chapters**;
  the last 40 are by **Gao E** (with **Cheng Weiyuan**), 1791. The 80-chapter manuscripts carry
  **Zhiyanzhai ("Red Inkstone")** commentary. **Redology (*hongxue*)**.
- Translations: **David Hawkes and John Minford** (*Story of the Stone*, Latinate names such as
  **Adamantina** = Miaoyu); **Yang Xianyi and Gladys Yang** (*A Dream of Red Mansions*).
- ★ **Frame:**
  - Nüwa used **36,500** stones; one, left at **Greensickness Peak**, is carried into the world by a
    **Buddhist monk and a Daoist priest**.
  - **Zhen Shiyin / Jia Yucun** — puns on **true (*zhen*) / false (*jia*)**: "Truth becomes fiction when
    the fiction's true."
  - Baoyu tours the **Land of Illusion** with the **Fairy Disenchantment** and has an erotic dream in
    **Qin Keqing's** room. Qin Keqing, who had an affair with her father-in-law **Jia Zhen**, dies.
- ★ **Lin Daiyu** — the **Crimson Pearl Flower** repaying a debt of watering with **tears**. Sickly and
  poetic; her **flower burial** and its poem ("who will bury me?"). She dies as Baoyu is tricked into
  marrying **Xue Baochai**, whose **golden locket** matches his jade ("gold and jade").
- **Wang Xifeng ("Phoenix")** — the scheming household manager; drives **Second Sister You** to
  **swallow gold**.
- **Jia Yuanchun**, eldest of the **four "Springs,"** becomes an **imperial consort**; the **Grand View
  (Prospect) Garden** is built for her visit.
- Other key figures: **Grandmother Jia**; the strict father **Jia Zheng**, who beats Baoyu; rustic
  **Granny Liu**; the maids **Aroma** and **Skybright**.
- Other episodes:
  - The **Crab-Flower (Begonia) Poetry Club**; the **chrysanthemum poems**.
  - The **embroidered purse** scandal and search; a begonia **blooming out of season**.
  - Baoyu **passes the exam (7th)** and vanishes with the monk and priest.

## The Classics

- ★ **Five Classics (*Wujing*)** — given official chairs (erudites) by **Emperor Wu of Han** (136 BC). A lost
  sixth, the ***Classic of Music***.
  - ★ ***Book of Songs / Classic of Poetry (Shijing)*** — **305 poems** in four parts: **Airs of the
    States (*Guofeng*)**, Minor Odes, Major Odes, Hymns. The first is **"Guanju"** (the **ospreys**).
    Tradition says **Confucius** selected them.
  - ***Book of Documents (Shangshu)*** — the **New Text / Old Text** controversy; the "Old Text" chapters
    were forged.
  - ★ ***I Ching (Yijing; Book of Changes; Zhouyi)***:
    - **64 hexagrams** of six broken/unbroken lines; the **Ten Wings** commentaries are credited to
      Confucius.
    - Read through **Leibniz** (binary), **Jung** (foreword to the **Wilhelm** translation), and **John
      Cage** (*Music of Changes*, chance operations).
  - ***Book of Rites (Liji)*** — compiled by **Dai Sheng**; contains the *Great Learning* and *Doctrine of
    the Mean*.
  - ★ ***Spring and Autumn Annals (Chunqiu)*** — Lu, **722–481 BC**. Terse entries read for "**praise and
    blame**." Three commentaries: **Zuo zhuan** (Zuo Qiuming), **Gongyang**, **Guliang**.
- ★ **Four Books (*Sishu*)** — fixed by **Zhu Xi** (1190); the **imperial exam** syllabus from **1313
  (Yuan) to 1905**.
  - ★ ***Analects (Lunyu)*** — opens "Is it not a pleasure to learn and practise what is learned?"
  - ★ ***Mencius*** — human nature is good; the **four sprouts**. Mencius's mother moved house
    **three times** for his education.
  - ***Great Learning*** — the **eight steps**, from the **investigation of things (*gewu*)** to peace
    under heaven.
  - ***Doctrine of the Mean (Zhongyong)***.
- **Thirteen Classics** — the Five Classics expanded (three Rites texts, three *Annals* commentaries) plus
  the *Analects*, *Mencius*, *Classic of Filial Piety*, and the dictionary ***Erya***.
