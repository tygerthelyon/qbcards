# Zhou Dynasty — qbreader supplement

*The gaps in my Zhou notes (Western Zhou, Spring and Autumn, Warring States), from about 1,160 qbreader
questions. In quizbowl the Zhou is asked mostly through its **philosophers**. The top answer lines are
Confucius (38), Daoism (~45), Zhuangzi (12), Mencius (11), the Analects, Laozi, and the* Art of War*, well
ahead of the dynasty itself (16). My notes give the Hundred Schools one line, so most of this section is
philosophy.*

*★ = recurring quizbowl clue.*

## Corrections to my notes
- **Vertical and horizontal alliances are swapped.**
  - ***Hezong* = "vertical" (north–south)**: the six states allying **against Qin**, organised by **Su Qin**.
  - ***Lianheng* = "horizontal" (east–west)**: states allying **with Qin**, promoted by **Zhang Yi**.
- **Rebellion of the Three Guards:** the Duke of Zhou didn't rebel. He served as **regent** for the boy
  **King Cheng**. Guanshu, Caishu, and Huoshu rebelled against **his regency** (with the Shang heir Wugeng),
  and he put them down.
- **Spring and Autumn period** = **770–476 BC** (from the move east to Luoyang). The *Annals* themselves
  cover 722–481.
- ***Xian* (counties)** do date from the Warring States, but ***sheng* (provinces)** are much later (Yuan
  dynasty).
- **Bai Qi** served the **state** of Qin; the Qin *dynasty* starts only in 221 BC.

## Western Zhou
- ★ **Houji** was the Zhou ancestor. **King Wen** (Ji Chang), held prisoner by the Shang, is credited with
  arranging the *I Ching*'s 64 hexagrams (the "King Wen sequence"). His son **King Wu** won at **Muye**.
- ★ **Duke of Zhou** (Ji Dan):
  - Regent for King Cheng, then **stepped aside** — the Confucian model minister. Confucius lamented he no
    longer dreamed of him: "I am for the Zhou!"
  - Built the eastern capital **Chengzhou (Luoyang)** for the defeated Shang elite, and moved the
    **Nine Tripods** there.
  - Codified **fengjian** and the "**rites and music**." Credited with the ***Rites of Zhou*** (360 posts
    under six offices) and the dream book ***Duke of Zhou's Interpretation of Dreams***.
  - In the *Book of Documents* chapter **"The Metal-Bound Coffer,"** he offers his life for his sick brother.
  - First to formulate the ★ **Mandate of Heaven**: natural disasters can reflect a ruler's faults.
- **King Mu** drove his **eight steeds** west to meet the **Queen Mother of the West** (*Mu Tianzi Zhuan*).
- **Well-field system (*jingtian*)** — a square divided into nine like the character 井. Eight families
  farmed the outer plots and together worked the central one for the lord. Mencius idealised it; Wang Mang
  tried to revive it.
- ★ **King You** and **Bao Si**: he **lit the beacon fires** as a false alarm to make her laugh. When the
  **Quanrong** (with the Marquess of Shen) really attacked in **771 BC**, no one came. He was killed, and
  King Ping moved the capital east.
- Ritual bronzes with **long inscriptions** (the **Mao Gong ding**) record Zhou investitures. Iron and
  chariot warfare spread over the period.

## Spring and Autumn (770–476 BC)
- ★ The ***ba*** (hegemon) system — **Duke Huan of Qi** was the first, advised by ★ **Guan Zhong**, who
  is credited with salt and iron monopolies and a namesake book, the ***Guanzi***.
  - The usual **Five Hegemons**: Huan of Qi, **Wen of Jin**, Zhuang of Chu, Mu of Qin, and Xiang of Song
    (some lists swap in **Goujian** and **Fuchai**).
  - **Duke Wen of Jin's** follower **Jie Zhitui** cut flesh from his own thigh to feed him in exile. When
    Jie burned to death in the forest set alight to flush him out, the **Cold Food Festival** began.
  - Battles: **Chengpu** (632 BC; Jin beats Chu).
- ★ **Wu and Yue**:
  - **Wu Zixu**, ordered to kill himself by **King Fuchai** of Wu, asked that his **eyes be hung on the
    city gate** to watch Yue conquer Wu.
  - **King Goujian** of Yue "**slept on brushwood and tasted gall**" (*woxin changdan*) to keep his
    revenge alive, then destroyed Wu (473 BC).
  - Goujian's minister **Fan Li** sent **Xi Shi** to Fuchai, and later retired with her onto a boat on
    **Lake Tai** — he became a model merchant ("**twelve golden rules**" of business).
- ★ The **partition of Jin** (403 BC) by the **Han, Zhao, and Wei** families ends the period.
- ★ ***Zuo zhuan*** (Zuo Qiuming) — the great narrative commentary on the *Spring and Autumn Annals*, the
  source of many proverbs. The other two are the **Gongyang** and **Guliang**.
  - The **Gongyang** school's **Dong Zhongshu** wrote ***Luxuriant Dew of the Spring and Autumn Annals***,
    merging Confucianism with five-phase cosmology.

## Warring States (475–221 BC)
- **Seven powers:** Qin, Chu, Qi, Yan, Han, Zhao, and Wei.
- ★ **Shang Yang**:
  - A **Wei** native who worked for **Duke Xiao of Qin**. His reforms: **ranks of merit** won in battle,
    collective responsibility, **counties (*xian*)**, abolition of the **well-field** system, and
    **standardised weights**.
  - Theories of "**fixing the standards**" and "**treating the people as one**"; the ***Book of Lord Shang***.
  - Executed by being **torn apart by chariots** after his patron died.
- ★ **Jixia Academy** in Qi's capital Linzi — the state-funded salon of the Hundred Schools. The **Four Lords**
  (e.g., **Lord Mengchang** of Qi) kept thousands of retainers.
- ★ **Qu Yuan** — the Chu minister-poet, author of ***Li Sao*** ("Encountering Sorrow") in the ***Chu Ci***
  (*Songs of the South*). Exiled, he **drowned himself in the Miluo River**. The **Dragon Boat Festival** and
  ***zongzi*** commemorate the search for his body.
- **Battle of Changping** (260 BC): Bai Qi buried ~400,000 surrendered Zhao soldiers. Zhao's general
  **Zhao Kuo** was the proverbial "**armchair strategist**" who only knew books.
- **Dujiangyan** was built by **Li Bing** (and his son) for Qin, around 256 BC.
- **Marquis Yi of Zeng's tomb** (c. 433 BC) held a set of **65 bronze bells (*bianzhong*)**.

## The Hundred Schools of Thought

### ★ Confucius (Kong Qiu; Kongzi; 551–479 BC)
- From **Qufu, Lu**. He was a minor official (minister of crime), then made a **13-year tour** of the
  states and taught **72 disciples** the **Six Arts**.
- Core ideas:
  - ***ren*** (humaneness): "restraining yourself and returning to the rites constitutes *ren*"
  - ***li*** (ritual), ***xiao*** (filial piety), the ***junzi*** (gentleman), the **five relationships**
  - ★ the "**rectification of names**": "let the ruler be a ruler…"
- Stock clues:
  - He condemned the **Ji family's eight rows of dancers** (a royal prerogative) and the **music of Zheng**.
  - "I am for the Zhou!"
  - The **Silver Rule**: "Do not impose on others what you yourself do not desire."
- Afterlife:
  - Emperor Wu made Confucianism state orthodoxy.
  - The **Temple of Confucius** in Qufu: the **Hall of Great Perfection** and the **Apricot Platform**.
  - **Red Guards** smashed his cemetery in 1966. Mao's **Criticize Lin, Criticize Confucius** campaign
    followed in 1973–74.
  - **Confucius Institutes** (the first in Seoul, 2004); Taiwan's **Teachers' Day** (Sept 28).
  - Herbert Fingarette's ***Confucius: The Secular as Sacred***.

### ★ Mencius (Meng Ke; the "Second Sage")
- Human nature is **good**: the **four sprouts** (compassion, shame, deference, right and wrong) grow
  into **ren, yi, li, zhi**.
- ★ The **child about to fall into a well** — anyone would feel alarm.
- ★ The king who **spared an ox** (substituting a sheep) should "extend" that compassion to his people.
- ★ Conquest is "**climbing a tree to look for fish**."
- The **Ox Mountain** parable (stripped bare, but its nature was forested). Water prefers to flow **down**,
  as people tend to good.
- ★ **Mencius's mother moved three times** for his education, and **cut her weaving** when he played truant.
- Studied under the school of **Zisi**. Attacked **Yang Zhu** ("would not pluck one hair to benefit the
  world") and **Mozi**. Justified **overthrowing a tyrant**.

### Xunzi
- Human nature is **bad** ("crooked wood needs steaming"); goodness comes from ritual and teaching.
- Taught the Legalists **Han Fei** and **Li Si**.

### ★ Laozi and the *Daodejing* (Tao Te Ching)
- **Laozi**, a Zhou **archivist**, rode west on a **water buffalo**. The **gatekeeper Yin Xi** made him
  write down his teaching (**5,000 characters**).
  - Deified as **Taishang Laojun**, lowest of the Three Pure Ones; in *Journey to the West* he cooks Sun
    Wukong in his furnace.
  - He is the smiling one in the "**Vinegar Tasters**" painting.
- The text: **81 chapters** (37 *Dao* + 44 *De*). It opens: "The **Dao that can be told is not the
  eternal Dao**; the name that can be named is not the eternal name."
  - Key ideas: ★ ***wuwei*** (non-action), ***ziran*** (naturalness), the "**uncarved block**" (*pu*),
    the "**mysterious female**," heaven and earth treating creatures as "**straw dogs**," a **wheel's**
    use is in its **empty hub**, "a journey of a thousand *li* begins with a single step."
- **Wang Bi** wrote the classic commentary (Neo-Daoism, *Xuanxue*). The 1973 **Mawangdui** silk copies have
  the two parts in reverse order.

### ★ Zhuangzi
- ★ The **butterfly dream**: is he a man dreaming he's a butterfly, or a butterfly dreaming he's a man?
  That is "the transformation of things."
- ★ **Cook Ding** (Butcher Ding), whose knife hasn't dulled in **19 years** because he cuts between the joints.
- ★ The **happiness of fish** debate with his friend **Huizi (Hui Shi)** on a bridge over the Hao River.
- **Drumming on a basin and singing** at his wife's death.
- The **useless tree** that lives long; the **turtle** that would rather drag its tail in the mud than
  be enshrined.
- **Hundun** (Chaos) dies when friends drill seven holes in him.
- The "**pipes of heaven**" (piping of earth, man, heaven). The book is divided into the **Inner (7),
  Outer, and Miscellaneous chapters**.
- **Liezi** — another Daoist book; Laozi and Zhuangzi are the founders.

### ★ Mozi and Mohism
- **Mozi**, possibly a **craftsman and fortification expert**, defended small states against siege (he
  outwitted **Lu Ban**'s siege engines). Only **defensive war** is just.
- ★ **"Universal love" / impartial care (*jian'ai*)**, against Confucian graded love.
- The **Ten Doctrines** (in "triplets" of chapters): **against music**, **moderation in funerals**,
  **against fatalism**, **elucidating ghosts** (encourage belief in ghosts for social order), **Heaven's
  will** as a measuring standard (*fa*).
- The "three tests" (basis, verifiability, applicability) make it an early **(state) consequentialism**.
- The later **Mohist canon** has optics, mechanics, and logic.
- Its offshoot was the ★ **School of Names (Logicians)**:
  - **Gongsun Long**: "**a white horse is not a horse**."
  - **Hui Shi**'s paradoxes: "I set off for Yue today and arrived yesterday"; a one-foot stick halved
    daily is never used up.

### ★ Legalism (*Fajia*)
- **Shang Yang** (see above).
- ★ **Han Fei** (*Han Feizi*):
  - A stuttering prince of **Han**, taught by Xunzi. Made to **drink poison** in a Qin prison by his
    fellow student **Li Si**.
  - The "**two handles**" of reward and punishment. The "**Five Vermin**" of the state (scholars,
    speechmakers, swordsmen, draft-dodgers, merchants).
  - The **stump-watcher**: a farmer waiting for another hare to break its neck on his stump, i.e.
    imitators of antiquity.
  - The **unbeatable spear and impenetrable shield**, the origin of the word for contradiction, ***maodun***.
- Shen Buhai (technique, *shu*) and Shen Dao (positional power, *shi*).

### Others
- **Yang Zhu** — egoism ("not one hair").
- **Agriculturalists** (Xu Xing; Shennong) — everyone, kings included, should farm.
- **Yin-yang / Naturalists** — **Zou Yan**'s five-phase cycles of dynasties.

### ★ Sun Tzu, *The Art of War*
- **13 chapters**. It opens "War is a matter of **vital importance to the State**." The **five constant
  factors** are the Moral Law, Heaven, Earth, the Commander, and Method.
- "**All warfare is based on deception**"; "**know the enemy and know yourself**."
- The **Nine Situations**; **five kinds of spies** (the last chapter); **attack by fire**.
- Commentary by **Cao Cao**. A **1972 Yinqueshan** tomb find also produced **Sun Bin's** *Art of War*,
  settling an authorship dispute.
- Translated by the Jesuit **Jean-Joseph Amiot** (1772). Story: Sun Tzu **beheaded two royal concubines**
  to make the king of **Wu**'s palace ladies drill properly.
