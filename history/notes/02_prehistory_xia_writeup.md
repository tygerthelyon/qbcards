*★ = recurring quizbowl clue. Prehistory is rarely the **answer** to a tossup; it shows up as clues

### What changed from my notes
- **Liangzhu** was in the **lower Yangtze** (around Lake Tai, Zhejiang), not the middle Yellow River.
- **Longshan** is **late Neolithic** (c. 3000–1900 BC); **Yangshao** is the middle Neolithic.
- **Jiahu** (Peiligang culture) is in the **Huai River** basin, Henan. It is known for **rice**, not as the
  first millet farmers. The early millet cultures are **Cishan** and **Peiligang**.
- That **Yangshao** spread the Sino-Tibetan languages is a **hypothesis** (from genetics and linguistics),
  not an established fact.
- "Erligang… established by Pan Geng… site of its final capital" mixed two places. **Erligang = Zhengzhou**,
  an early Shang capital. **Pan Geng** moved the capital to **Yin (Yinxu, Anyang)**, the last one, where
  the oracle bones are.

## Prehistory

### Early humans
- ***Homo erectus*** ("upright man") reached East Asia **1.3–1.8 million years ago**. Its type specimen is
  ★ **Java Man** (Eugène Dubois, Trinil). **Yuanmou Man** (Yunnan) is among the oldest Chinese
  *H. erectus* finds, at about **1.7 million years**; **Lantian Man** (Shaanxi) is another.
- ★ **Peking Man** (*Homo erectus pekinensis*; *Sinanthropus pekinensis*):
  - Lived about **780,000–400,000 years ago**, at ★ **Zhoukoudian** ("Dragon Bone Hill") southwest of
    Beijing.
  - **Johan Gunnar Andersson** first found teeth there (1921). The 1920s–30s digs were under **Davidson
    Black**, who named it, with **Pei Wenzhong** (who found the first skull, 1929) and the Jesuit
    ★ **Pierre Teilhard de Chardin**.
  - **Franz Weidenreich** made the casts. The **original fossils vanished in 1941** while being
    shipped to the US, so only the casts survive.
  - Used in China as evidence for a **multiregional** (not "out of Africa") origin of the Chinese, and in
    Marxist accounts of "primitive communism."
  - Possibly used **fire**.
- Later archaic humans: **Dali Man** (skull, 1978, Shaanxi); the **Harbin "Dragon Man" skull**, now
  ***Homo longi*** and linked in 2025 to the ★ **Denisovans** (first found in the **Denisova Cave**,
  Altai; Denisovan genes such as **EPAS1** help **Tibetans** live at altitude).
- **Fuyan Cave** (Dao County, Hunan) — modern-human teeth claimed at **80,000–120,000 years**,
  contested. **Tianyuan Man** (near Zhoukoudian, c. 40,000 years) is a securely dated early modern human.

### Neolithic firsts
- The Neolithic in China begins about **10,000 years ago**, with **two farming systems**: **millet** in the
  north (Yellow River) and **rice** in the south (Yangtze).
- ★ **Xianrendong** ("Immortals' Cave," Jiangxi) has the **world's oldest pottery** (c. **20,000** years
  ago) and early rice use. **Shangshan** (Zhejiang) has early domesticated rice (c. 9,000–10,000 years).
- **Jiahu** (c. 7000–5700 BC; **Peiligang culture**; Henan):
  - ★ **Jiahu flutes** of **red-crowned crane wing bones** — among the **oldest playable instruments**.
  - ★ **Jiahu symbols** on **tortoise shells** — possible proto-writing, the forerunners of the Shang
    oracle bones.
  - The **oldest known fermented drink**, of rice, honey, and hawthorn/grape (Patrick McGovern; "Chateau
    Jiahu").
  - Abandoned after a flood, after about 1,300 years.
- **Peiligang** and **Cishan** (c. 7000–5000 BC) — the earliest **millet** farmers of the Yellow River.
- **Damaidi** (Zhongwei, Ningxia) — thousands of **petroglyphs**, some argued to be proto-characters.

### The big Neolithic cultures

| Culture | Dates | Where | Known for |
|---|---|---|---|
| ★ **Yangshao** | 5000–3000 BC | middle Yellow River (Henan, Shaanxi) | **painted pottery**; type site **Banpo** |
| **Majiayao** | 3300–2000 BC | upper Yellow River (Gansu, Qinghai) | the finest **painted pottery** (Banshan, Machang phases); **China's oldest bronze knife** (c. 3000 BC) |
| ★ **Hongshan** | 4700–2900 BC | **Liao River**, northeast | **jade "pig-dragons"**; the Goddess Temple at **Niuheliang** |
| **Dawenkou** | 4100–2600 BC | Shandong | grave goods marking rank; ancestor of Longshan |
| **Hemudu** | 5000–4500 BC | south of Hangzhou Bay | **stilt houses**, wet rice, early lacquer |
| ★ **Liangzhu** | 3300–2300 BC | **lower Yangtze** (Lake Tai) | **jade *cong* and *bi***; a walled city with huge dams |
| ★ **Longshan** | 3000–1900 BC | middle/lower Yellow River | **black eggshell pottery** on the **potter's wheel**; rammed-earth walls; Taosi and Shimao |
| **Qijia** | 2200–1600 BC | upper Yellow River | early **bronze**; **Lajia** |

- ★ **Yangshao** ("Yangshao village," Henan):
  - Discovered **1921** by the Swedish geologist **Johan Gunnar Andersson**, who first linked it to
    Turkmenistan's **Anau** culture.
  - ★ **Banpo** (near Xi'an) — a **moated village** of round houses, **pottery kilns**, **children buried in
    urns**, and the **Banpo symbols**.
  - Phases: Banpo, **Miaodigou**, Late Yangshao.
  - Grew **millet**, raised pigs, and cultivated **hemp** and **mulberry**.
- **Majiayao** — sometimes treated as a western branch of Yangshao.
- ★ **Hongshan** — coexisted with Yangshao; its **pig-dragons** are among the earliest Chinese dragons.
- ★ **Liangzhu** (Zhejiang):
  - The last great **jade** culture and one of East Asia's first **states**.
  - ★ ***Cong*** (square tubes, earth) and ***bi*** (round discs, heaven), shaped with abrasive sand and
    carved with a **mask motif**. Shang royals such as Fu Hao collected them.
  - A huge walled city with China's oldest **hydraulic dams** (a UNESCO site).
  - Declined during the **4.2-kiloyear event**, the same drought that ended the Akkadian Empire.
- ★ **Longshan** ("Dragon Mountain," Shandong):
  - **Black eggshell pottery**, thrown on the **potter's wheel** (which Yangshao lacked);
    **rammed-earth** (*hangtu*) town walls; scapulimancy (burning bones to divine).
  - **Taosi** (Shanxi) — a walled city with possibly the **earliest astronomical observatory**, often
    linked to the legendary **Yao**.
  - **Shimao** (Shaanxi) — a stone city with **jade set into its walls** and skulls under its gate.
  - Longshan leads into the **Erlitou** culture.
- **Qijia** — some of China's first **copper/bronze** objects and domestic horses.
  - Its **Lajia** site (Qinghai) was buried by an earthquake and a flood around **1920 BC**, preserving
    the world's **oldest noodles** (millet).
  - A 2016 study tied this to an outburst flood from the **Jishi Gorge** on the Yellow River, a candidate
    for the real **Great Flood of Yu**.

### Bronze Age sites outside the Central Plain
- ★ **Sanxingdui** ("Three Star Mound," near Guanghan, **Sichuan**), c. 1600–1000 BC:
  - A bronze culture identified with the ancient kingdom of ★ **Shu**, whose legendary founder **Cancong**
    had "protruding eyes."
  - **Sacrificial pits** hold **bronze masks with protruding, cylindrical eyes**, **gold-foil masks**, a
    **2.6 m standing figure**, a **bronze tree** about 4 m tall, and **elephant tusks**.
  - It developed bronze **independently** of the Shang style and knew nothing of writing.
  - Succeeded by **Jinsha** (Chengdu), with its **Golden Sun Bird** disc (the emblem of Chinese
    heritage) and a **smiling gold mask**.
- **Zhangzhung** (c. 500 BC–625 AD) — the kingdom of **western Tibet** (around Mount Kailash), cradle of the
  ★ **Bon** religion; conquered by **Songtsen Gampo**, founder of the **Tibetan Empire**.

## Xia Dynasty

- Traditional dates **2070–1600 BC**, from the ★ **Xia–Shang–Zhou Chronology Project** (1996–2000).
  **17 kings in 14 generations**.
- Contemporary with the Minoans, the Indus Valley Civilisation, Egypt's Middle Kingdom, and the writing
  of *Gilgamesh*.
- Known only from much later texts — ★ **Sima Qian's *Shiji*** (*Records of the Grand Historian*) and the
  ★ ***Bamboo Annals***. The *Bamboo Annals* were found in **281 AD** in the tomb of a Warring States king
  of **Wei**. **No Xia writing has ever been found**, so many treat the dynasty as semi-legendary.
- ★ The *Shiji*'s **Shang** king list was **confirmed by the Anyang oracle bones**, which is the main
  argument for taking its Xia list seriously too.
- ★ **Erlitou culture** (c. 1900–1500 BC; Yanshi, near **Luoyang**, Henan) — the leading candidate for the
  Xia (or its capital):
  - **Palace foundations** of **rammed earth**; the **earliest Chinese ritual bronzes** (*jue* wine
    vessels), cast in **piece-moulds**; a **turquoise-inlaid dragon**.
  - Erlitou is the earliest stage of the Yellow River **bronze** tradition; it was followed by the
    Shang **Erligang** culture (Zhengzhou).

### Kings and stories
- ★ **Yu the Great (Da Yu; "Yu the Engineer")** — tamed the **Great Flood** with **canals and dredging**
  instead of his father **Gun's** dams (and Gun's stolen *xirang* soil).
  - **13 years**; passed his own door **three times** without going in.
  - Cast the ★ **Nine Tripods (*Jiuding*)** for the **nine provinces** — the symbol of legitimate rule,
    later moved by each new dynasty (Tang of Shang moved them).
  - Received the **Luoshu** diagram from a turtle in the Luo River. Traditionally credited with the
    *Classic of Mountains and Seas*.
  - **Shun abdicated** to him for merit.
- ★ **Qi**, Yu's son, took the throne, and **hereditary succession** replaced abdication by merit. That
  makes the Xia "China's first dynasty."
- **Taikang** lost the throne to **(Hou) Yi of Youqiong**, a usurping archer (not the sun-shooter, though
  the legends blur). **Shaokang** restored the line — the **Restoration of Shaokang**.
- ★ **Jie (Lü Gui)**, the last king — the model **tyrant**. His concubine **Mo Xi** (my notes' "cruel
  woman") loved the sound of **tearing silk**; he built a **wine pool**.
  - ★ Defeated by **Tang of Shang (Cheng Tang; Zi Lü)** at the ★ **Battle of Mingtiao** (c. 1600 BC) and
    exiled to Nanchao.
  - The Shang used Jie's fall as the **first case of losing Heaven's favour**, the template for the
    later **Mandate of Heaven** (a Zhou concept).
- The *Bamboo Annals*' omens at the fall of the Xia (**a yellow fog, a dim sun, three suns, frost in
  summer**) have been linked by Robert Ritner and others to the **Thera (Minoan) eruption** that buried
  **Akrotiri**.
- **Rammed earth (*hangtu*; pisé)** — layers of earth pounded in wooden frames. It built the Longshan
  and Erlitou walls and palaces, and later the Shang walls of Zhengzhou and the early Great Wall.
