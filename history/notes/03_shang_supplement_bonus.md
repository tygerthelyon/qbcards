### Correction to my notes
- "Erligang… established by Pan Geng, was the site of its final capital" — **Erligang = Zhengzhou**, an
  early capital (Ao/Bo). **Pan Geng** moved the capital to **Yin (Yinxu, near Anyang)**, the last one. The
  later Shang is often called the **Yin dynasty** from Pan Geng on.

### Founding
- ★ The ancestor myth: **Jiandi swallowed an egg dropped by a black bird** and bore **Xie**, the first Shang
  ancestor.
- ★ **Tang** (Cheng Tang; Tang the Sage) beat **Jie** at **Mingtiao** and **moved the Nine Tripods**, the
  dynasty's founding act.
  - A drought of five or seven years followed. Tang offered himself as a sacrifice in the **Mulberry
    Forest**, and the rain came.
  - ★ Tang's **bathtub inscription**, "make it new" (日新), became **Ezra Pound's** modernist slogan.
  - His minister **Yi Yin**, legendarily a cook, later exiled Tang's grandson Tai Jia to reform him.

### Kings, Fu Hao, and the late Shang
- ★ **Wu Ding** — the great late king, and the source of most surviving oracle-bone inscriptions. His
  consort ★ **Fu Hao** (Lady Hao):
  - Led armies against the **Tufang, Guifang, Qiang, and Ba**.
  - Her tomb (found **1976** at Anyang, excavated by **Zheng Zhenxiang**) is the only intact Shang royal
    tomb. It held over 1,600 objects, much Liangzhu-era **jade**, and **16 human sacrifices**.
  - The oracle bones record Wu Ding worrying over her childbirth.
- **Zu Jia** — instituted ritual reforms that later texts never mention (known only from the oracle bones).
- **Wu Yi** — "shot at Heaven" by firing arrows at a **leather bag of blood**, and was **killed by
  lightning**.
- ★ **Di Xin (King Zhou)**:
  - The **wine pool and meat forest**; the **Deer Terrace**, where he **burned himself to death** after
    **Muye**.
  - The ***paolao*** — prisoners walked a **greased bronze pillar over coals**, at **Daji's**
    suggestion. Daji's head was hung on a **white flag** after the fall.
  - He **cut out the heart of his uncle Bigan** to see whether a sage's heart has seven openings.
  - His half-brother **Weizi** went over to the Zhou and founded the state of **Song**, so the Shang royal
    line continued there. **Confucius** claimed descent from it.
  - **Jizi** (Kija) supposedly went to Korea and founded **Gija Joseon**.
- ★ **Battle of Muye (1046 BC)** — **King Wu of Zhou** and **Jiang Ziya** defeat the Shang. The Zhou
  justified it with the ★ **Mandate of Heaven**, the first time it was used.

### Oracle bones (*jiaguwen*)
- ★ **Turtle plastrons and ox scapulae**. A question was carved, the bone **heated with a hot point until it
  cracked**, and the crack read. The character **卜 (*bu*)** pictures the crack.
  - Entries run **preface, charge, prognostication, verification** (David Keightley, *Sources of Shang
    History*, 1978).
  - Topics include **toothache**, hunts, weather, wars, and **childbirth**.
  - The Shang's supreme god is **Di (Shangdi)**; "If we build a settlement, Di will not obstruct."
- ★ Rediscovered in **1899**: **Wang Yirong**, sick with **malaria**, saw characters on "**dragon bones**"
  bought from a pharmacy for medicine.
  - **Luo Zhenyu** traced them to Anyang. **Wang Guowei** used them to confirm the *Shiji* king list.
  - **Dong Zuobin** divided them into **five periods** (from Wu Ding). The Canadian missionary **James
    Mellon Menzies** collected and reassembled thousands.
- ★ Days are counted in the **sexagenary cycle**: **ten Heavenly Stems × twelve Earthly Branches** (*ganzhi*).
- The earliest **Chinese writing**. The **large seal script** grew out of it, and the hexagrams of the
  *I Ching* later replaced bone divination.

### Bronzes and sites
- ★ The ***taotie*** mask on ritual bronzes (*ding* tripods, *gui*, *jue*, *gong*, *zun*). **Max Loehr's**
  **five styles** of Shang bronze décor.
  - Cast in **piece-moulds**, not lost wax (lost wax is a later Zhou technique).
- ★ The **Houmuwu ding** (832 kg) — the heaviest bronze vessel of the ancient world, found at Anyang.
- **Yinxu** ("Ruins of Yin," Anyang): royal tombs with mass **human sacrifice** and chariot burials.
- **K. C. Chang** argued that the "Three Dynasties" (*Sandai*: Xia, Shang, Zhou) partly overlapped rather
  than simply following one another.
