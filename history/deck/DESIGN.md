# History deck v2 — design

What the research says, and what this deck does about it. Short on purpose.

## 1. The core card is a clue in, an answer out
A tossup gives clues and asks for one answer, so every core card does the same: one tossup-style clue on
the front ("This goddess…"), one short answer on the back. Retrieval practice transfers best when the
practice question has the same shape as the real one.

**Why not sentence cloze?** Cloze cards drift into **pattern-matching the sentence** rather than
retrieving the idea (Matuschak: cloze "seems to produce less understanding than question-answer
pairs"). Several clozes cut from one sentence also **give each other away**. Clozing a *non-answer*
word ("Pangu was born in an {{egg}}") trains the reverse direction, answer → clue, which a tossup never
asks for. So: no sentence clozes from the notes.

**Where cloze is right:** closed sets you must reproduce whole — the Five Classics, the Four Books, the
zodiac order, the Four Symbols, the Five Phases, the Eight Immortals. These are **QB List** cards: one
card per item, the *other items hidden* on the front (so they don't answer each other, the fix your
Geography List cards already got), the whole set revealed on the back.

## 2. Several clues per answer, from different angles
Retrieving the same thing through **varied cues** builds a memory that a new clue can reach (Butler
2010, 2017; PNAS 2024 on variable retrieval). An answer gets as many clue cards as quizbowl asks it:
Sun Wukong gets about nine, Kuafu one. Each clue is tagged by where it sits in a pyramid —
`pos::lead` (hard, early), `pos::mid`, `pos::give` (the giveaway). New cards are ordered **giveaway
first**, so you learn the easy door in and then the hard ones.

## 3. Train the confusable pairs on purpose
Similar items interfere (SuperMemo rule 11), and **interleaving similar categories** is what teaches
you to tell them apart (Kornell & Bjork 2008; Kang). Examples:
- Hou Yi the sun-shooter / Yi of Youqiong the usurper
- Nüwa the creator / Nüwa-Jingwei
- the *Sanguozhi* / the *Sanguo Yanyi*
- Lü Bu / Lü Buwei / Lü Meng
- Xuanzang / Xuanzong
- Zhu Bajie / Zhu Wuneng
- Sha Wujing / Sun Wukong

They get a **Not to be confused with** line on the back, and the worst ones get their own cards. New
cards are interleaved across entities, not fed in period by period.

## 4. Pictures: on the back by default, on the front only when the picture *is* the clue
A picture on the back costs nothing at recall and helps encoding (picture superiority). A picture on
the front is a real test only if quizbowl could describe it, and only if it doesn't print the answer.
So the Picture card exists only for things with a stock look:
- Nüwa and Fuxi's snake tails
- Guan Yu's red face and halberd
- Sun Wukong
- Wu Song and the tiger
- the Four Symbols roof tiles
- Daiyu burying flowers
- maps

Every picture shows **the card's answer**, never a stand-in from the same period (the fix from W105).

## 5. Pyramid practice: QB Tossup cards
Anki can't train buzzing, so for the most-asked answers there is a **QB Tossup** card. Clues are revealed
one at a time, from the hardest lead-in to the giveaway. Grade it by **where you could have buzzed**:
- **Easy** — on the first clue
- **Good** — mid-question
- **Hard** — the giveaway
- **Again** — not at all

## 6. Flash cards stay flash cards
One fact to recall per card, answers under ~40 characters, and an Extra line that is read, never
recalled (W63). The house look carries over:
- paper #f7f3ea, ink #2b2b2b, brown accent #6b4c3b; Optima for text, a serif for the answer
- a tier pill, and a proper night palette

The answer is set like an **answerline**: the required part underlined, with an *accept:* line.

## 7. Scheduling
- **FSRS on, desired retention 0.90**. Above 0.90 the workload climbs steeply; 0.80–0.95 is the sane
  range.
- Cards from my notes are active (I've verified and studied them). Supplement cards are active at tier 1–2
  and suspended at tier 3–4, like the other decks.
- **Leeches** (8 lapses): don't drill harder — **rewrite the card**. The export script gives lapse
  counts for exactly this.
